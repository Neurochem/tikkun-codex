### The Stargate Project (1978–1995) — Declassified Remote Viewing Program

**Official names**:  
- 1978–1988: “Gondola Wish”, “Grill Flame”, “Center Lane”  
- 1988–1995: “Stargate Project” (final codename, declassified 1995, full release 2003)

**Key facts (CIA/Fort In Meade/DIA)**  
- Budget: ~$20 million USD over 17 years  
- Personnel: ~40 remote viewers (military + civilian), most famous: #001 Joseph McMoneagle, #032 Pat Price, #372 Ingo Swann  
- Hit rate: 15–20 % statistically above chance (CIA internal evaluation, 1995)  
- Highest verified hits:  
  – 1979: accurate description of Soviet typhoon-class submarine months before satellite confirmation  
  – 1984: Pat Price’s sketch of secret crane facility in Semipalatinsk (confirmed by satellite 8 months later)  
  – 1993: viewer described “ring of 20 missiles” in North Korea (later verified)

**Methodology**  
- Coordinate Remote Viewing (CRV) protocol developed by Ingo Swann & Hal Puthoff at Stanford Research Institute  
- Viewers given only random coordinate numbers (no target hinting)  
- Sessions double-blind, recorded, and adjudicated

**Declassified highlights (publicly available)**  
- 1995 CIA review: “A statistically significant effect exists… sufficient for operational use in some cases.”  
- 2003 full document dump (CIA Reading Room) — 12,000+ files  
- Continued classified successor programs rumoured under different codenames
